    How Wellness & Longevity Care Works in Torrance

    Wellness and longevity care here means seven specific treatments, each tied to a goal such as chronic pain, a suspected parasitic infection, or interest in research-informed peptides. The first step is naming the goal and the symptoms behind it. Evidence comes next. Some options are FDA-approved drugs used off-label, and others are compounded and not FDA-approved. Primary care stays in the picture.

    Low Dose Naltrexone

    Naltrexone is FDA-approved at 50 mg for alcohol and opioid use disorders, while low dose naltrexone, usually 1.5 to 4.5 mg at bedtime, is prescribed off-label. Researchers believe a short receptor block and lower inflammation may explain its effects. Studies are small. Small studies in fibromyalgia and certain autoimmune conditions show promise but are not definitive. It cannot be taken with opioids.

    Naltrexone Gum in Torrance

    Naltrexone gum is a compounded form that delivers a small dose through the lining of the mouth, and some providers use it as a flexible, as-needed option to reduce cravings in specific situations. It is not FDA-approved. Research is limited. Your provider explains how and when to chew it, and how long to keep it in your mouth. Opioid use and liver problems are screened first.

    Antiparasitic Protocol, Ivermectin and Mebendazole

    Ivermectin is FDA-approved to treat certain parasitic worm infections such as strongyloidiasis and onchocerciasis, and it is also used for scabies and head lice. Mebendazole treats intestinal worms such as pinworm, roundworm, whipworm and hookworm. Travel history matters. Your provider picks the drug and dose based on the likely parasite and your exposure. These are prescribed only for their anti-parasitic uses.

    Anti-Aging and Immune Support Peptides in Torrance

    Peptides are short chains of amino acids that act as signaling molecules, and our Anti-Aging Peptides and Immune Support Peptides are compounded, research-informed therapies that are not FDA-approved for these uses. Some have small human trials, while others rely mostly on lab data. Most are injected. Expect a tiny needle under the skin a few times a week. FDA rules on compounded peptides can change availability.

    How a Provider Chooses Between Options

    Chronic pain or inflammation may point toward low dose naltrexone, while a suspected parasitic infection based on symptoms, travel or exposure points toward ivermectin, mebendazole or the Antiparasitic Protocol. Peptides come up when your goal is longevity or immune support and you accept the uncertainty. Safety comes first. Your provider weighs interactions, evidence and your preferences, and may recommend labs first. Goal decides the starting point.

    Side Effects and Safety in Torrance

    Low dose naltrexone may cause vivid dreams, insomnia or headache in the first weeks, which often ease or improve with morning dosing. Ivermectin can cause dizziness, nausea or itching, and mebendazole may cause stomach pain or diarrhea. Peptides vary. Expect possible redness at the injection site, plus peptide-specific effects your provider explains. Report anything unusual.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Your provider asks about your goal, symptoms, travel, opioid use, alcohol, liver and kidney health, pregnancy and every medication you take before recommending anything. Opioids of any kind, even a cough syrup with codeine, cannot be combined with naltrexone. Pregnancy changes options. Mebendazole and ivermectin are generally avoided in pregnancy unless clearly needed. Liver disease needs a closer look.
