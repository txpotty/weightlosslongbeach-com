    How Medical Weight Loss Works in Westminster

    Medical weight loss treats excess weight as a chronic condition. Your provider looks for causes like insulin resistance, untreated sleep apnea, thyroid problems or medicines known to add weight before building a plan. Medication may or may not be part of it. When medication fits, semaglutide and tirzepatide are frequent picks because they reduce appetite.

    Who Medical Weight Loss Is For

    Adults who have not reached a healthy weight with diet and exercise alone are the usual fit. Medication is typically an option at a BMI of 30 or above, or 27 or above with a condition tied to weight, such as sleep apnea, high cholesterol or type 2 diabetes. Coaching works below those cutoffs. Medication is not used during pregnancy.

    Treatment Schedule in Westminster

    Week one is the intake and review. A weekly injection plan steps up every four weeks, while a daily pill plan usually means one tablet each morning, sometimes building over a month. Check-ins come every few weeks early on. Stable patients often move to check-ins every month or two.

    What to Expect Week by Week

    The first two weeks focus on habits and, if prescribed, a starting dose. GLP-1 patients often feel less hungry by week two, and nausea or constipation tends to cluster right after the first dose and each step up. Expect slow, steady change. A loss of 1 to 2 pounds a week is a common target once a plan is underway, though medication plans vary.

    Side Effects in Westminster

    Side effects depend on the treatment. Injections tend to cause stomach symptoms, and stimulant pills tend to cause dry mouth, insomnia and a quicker pulse. Gallstones are a known risk. Protein at every meal and resistance exercise two or three times a week help protect muscle while you lose weight.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Screening covers your BMI, conditions, medications and weight history. Certain medicines are ruled out by a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN 2, uncontrolled high blood pressure, pregnancy or a history of eating disorders. Recent bloodwork helps. Common asks are A1C, a lipid panel, thyroid tests and a metabolic panel from the past year.

    Medical Weight Loss Compared With Other Approaches in Westminster

    Commercial diet programs cannot prescribe. They focus on food and behavior, while medical weight loss adds a licensed provider who can treat appetite hormones, manage related conditions and adjust medications that may be adding weight. Treatment options include FDA-approved injections, oral medicines and compounded versions that lack FDA approval. Bariatric surgery fits some higher BMIs.
